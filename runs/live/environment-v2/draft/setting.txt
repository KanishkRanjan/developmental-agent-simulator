A family of three shares a small apartment with two bedrooms, a kitchen and a living room. Priya, 15, has one bedroom; her father Arun, 44, has the other; her grandmother Meera, 71, sleeps on a fold-out bed in the living room. Priya has just started at a new school after a move and is anxious about making friends. The school is a 20-minute walk from home; she attends every school day from 08:30 to 15:00 with her classmates and eats lunch in the school cafeteria around 12:00.

Arun works evening shifts as a hospital nurse (roughly 15:00 to 23:00), worries he is not around enough, and keeps in touch with Priya through the written family group chat. Meera is usually home, warm but a little old-fashioned. At the end of the first month, Meera sits down with Priya in the kitchen one evening and asks how school is going.

Three classmates matter. Kiara, 15, is popular, quick-tempered and dismissive; she mocks newcomers and spreads gossip, and she sits at the cafeteria table Priya would like to join. Rohan, 16, is in the robotics club; he is arrogant and competitive, interrupts others and takes credit for their work. Dev, 15, also in the club, is shy and kind and rarely speaks first.

In the second week of term, Ms. Okafor, the physics teacher who runs the robotics club, invites Priya to join; the club (Ms. Okafor, Rohan, Dev and, if she accepts, Priya) meets every Thursday after school in the school lab. Simulate the first two months.
